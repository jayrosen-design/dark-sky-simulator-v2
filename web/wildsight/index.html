The problem

Wildlife collisions cost the U.S. $8.4 billion a year, and the signs aren't working.

Most collisions happen on ordinary two-lane roads, far from the few places that have fences or overpasses. The standard fix, a static yellow sign, is where almost none of the crashes happen.

How it works

Sleep, see, decide, warn, record.

WildSight spends almost all of its life asleep. It only spends energy, and only makes noise, when an animal is actually there. Click a step to replay it in the simulation at the top of the page.

The unit

Built from parts that already work.

Motion sensing, computer vision and frequency emitters are proven technologies. WildSight packages them in a weatherproof, solar-powered case that straps to a stake, a guardrail or a sign post, with service about once a year.

Hover a part to highlight it, explode the case, or switch the mount. The design follows the team's SolidWorks case, 3D-printed on a Bambu Lab printer.

    Species-tuned frequency

    Every species hears a different band. WildSight picks a tone near the top of the detected animal's range, above human hearing.

    Cited data

    Hearing ranges: Columbia Hearing; LSU "How well do dogs and other animals hear?" Preview shifts the tone into human hearing with the prototype app's mapping (200 + (kHz − 0.3) / 84.7 × 1800 Hz).

    Corridor mesh · roadmap

    Units along a road tell their neighbours when one detects an animal, so beacons light up before drivers reach the spot.

    Planned feature

    From the team's SIT "multiplication" concept: units embedded along lamps, signs and guardrails form a network. Spacing shown is illustrative.

    Driver beacon: the warning arrives earlier

    A driver who sees an amber flash starts braking before the animal is in the headlights. Compare stopping with and without the warning.

    Illustrative physics

    Assumes 1.5 s perception–reaction, 0.7 g braking on dry pavement, animal first visible 60 m ahead in low beams, beacon visible 150 m ahead. Stopping distance = v·t + v²/(2·a).

    Drivers told us they'd pay $100–300 for a wildlife detection system. Insurance agents and repair techs pointed to rural roads at dawn and dusk, in spring and fall.

    What the AI was trained on

    4,665 Kaggle images, transfer learning on MobileNet via Google Teachable Machine, exported to TensorFlow.js. Raccoons are a top-3 collision species with the thinnest data: the first thing to fix.

    What testing changed

    Removed after peer review: pheromone repellent (refills), AC power (cost), strobe light (driver distraction, now a softer blinking beacon), mounting on crossing signs (could disrupt migration).

    Next model: add people, vehicles and a "no animal" class; classify live video rather than stills.

    Next emitter: directional speaker aimed at the field, so nearby homes and drivers aren't exposed.

    Competitive landscape

    Nothing else detects, identifies and warns every driver from the roadside.

    Car-based systems protect one vehicle. Crossings protect one spot. Whistles have little evidence behind them. WildSight sits in the gap: cheap enough to spread along a corridor, smart enough to act per species.

    Roadmap

    From prototype to a standard roadside device.

    Stage 1 · now

    Develop & test

    • Functional unit with core features
    • Controlled tests: detection, deterrence, driver alerts
    • Refine per species with conservation experts
    • Open talks for regulatory approval
    Stage 2

    Pilot & awareness

    • Install units on a rural corridor in Alachua County
    • Measure animal behaviour and collisions
    • Public awareness with local authorities
    • Share pilot data with agencies
    Stage 3

    Commercialize

    • Production for state DOTs and private road operators
    • Work toward a standard collision-prevention device
    • Open sales to private landowners
    Stage 4

    National rollout

    • Installations in high-risk regions nationwide
    • Fleet data improves the AI models
    • Long-term ecological impact studies
    • Regular software and hardware updates
